# Checks on the physical ECP5-85F

Since October 22, 2026, the plan is to check remaining disagreements between sources and missing behavior rules on the available board. The general collection for the device continues; the absence of Diamond or of an exact model of an individual block does not stop it. This decision also applies to inconsistencies found later.

The available board was identified as an **ULX3S** on October 23, 2026. Board identification on 2026-10-29: USB product string "ULX3S FPGA 85K v3.0.8", JTAG IDCODE `0x41113043` (LFE5U-85), package CABGA381. The LED and FIRE-button pins are confirmed by the first observation below. The text marking on the package was not read. The next DSP/ALU bench is being prepared for this board after its pinout is clarified.

The board observations so far are listed under [Results](#results); every other series is still pending. 37 descriptions of series are prepared for EBR, PLC/LUTRAM, FF/CCU2, clocks/system and PIO/banks; the connection pins, the upload transport and the ready firmware for them are not chosen yet. The experiments are run as a separate stage after the bench is prepared. The [summary registry](inventory/LFE5U-85F/research_manifest.json) checks the references of the series to the 78 open questions.

## How to handle a disagreement before the measurement

A question keeps the family ID, the references to sources, the specific diverging statements and the status. The confirmed part of the structure can be used further. Missing behavior does not get a random constant, a convenient priority or the status "verified" because two tools agree.

A hardware experiment needs an initial state, controlled stimuli and an observation that distinguishes the possible explanations. If the observed values are the same for all hypotheses, such an experiment does not close the question. One experiment can clarify several IDs, but the structure, the tool conversion and the physical behavior are marked separately.

| Evidence level | What it confirms |
|---|---|
| Documented | A statement of a specific version of the vendor document |
| Tools checked | Connections, encodings and the added fabric in this version of the toolchain |
| Observed on the board | The actual result under the recorded conditions of the experiment |
| Repeated on the board | Reproducibility for the stated conditions, repetitions and instances |

For collisions that the vendor leaves undefined, an identical result over many repetitions remains an observation under specific conditions. It does not become a general priority of one port for all chips. When developing the digital model, the allowed uncertainty and the scope of the verified deterministic behavior must be recorded separately.

## What to keep with a result

For a run, the following is kept:

- The ID of the experiment and of the variant, the source HDL, the parameters, the INIT data and the expected differences between the hypotheses.
- The board model, the FPGA marking, the package and the revision, if known; what is unknown stays explicitly unfilled.
- The tool versions, the constraints, the placement, the connection table and the checksums of `.bit`/`.config`.
- The actually recovered MODE, widths, registers, control muxes, WID and INIT of the block under test.
- The sequence of startup, local reset/GSR, clocks and input values; the source and the phase of the clocks.
- The raw observations by steps or samples, the number of repetitions, capture errors and environment parameters, if they were measured.
- The conclusion with its scope of applicability, the new status of the question and a reference to the observations themselves.

The bench and the observer are also part of the circuit under test. For EBR it is important to distinguish direct control of the primitive from the environment that the synthesizer added to execute the source HDL. When checking reset/GSR, the observer must keep the ability to record the event itself; when checking an early start, it must have an independent means of observation and controlled EBR clocks.

To begin with, a digital comparison of samples with separated events is enough. Measurements near coinciding edges are made separately, with a description of how the phase is set; they do not require turning the whole emulator kernel into a fully timing-aware one.
